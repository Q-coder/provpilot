From the Alps at 1 m to the rivets on a Cessna.

ProVPilot is a free, open-source flight simulator built on JSBSim flight dynamics and the Godot engine. A base scenery covers nine countries from Norway to Italy with real terrain, airports and night lighting; photoreal 1 m add-ons for Switzerland and Baden-Württemberg built from swisstopo and LGL BW data are being reworked and will return. Pick a Cessna, a PC-7, an A-10, a glider or an MD-80, pick an airport, and go.

What is ProVPilot?

A no-friction flight simulator focused on the Alps and Europe. The goal: accurate 6-DOF flight physics in faithful aircraft, flown over the actual landscape — real terrain elevations, real airports with their runways, taxiways and lights, and, where the add-ons are installed, the same orthophotos, buildings, roads and rivers as the real world — all streamed at runtime from a custom C++ Godot extension.

Originally a Godot 3 demo by chunky/jsbgodot, ProVPilot has been rewritten from the ground up for Godot 4.7 with a custom double-precision engine fork. Since 1.0.20 the world is a true WGS84 globe: no floating-origin tricks, no jitter at coordinate extremes, a 200 km+ visible horizon, and a flight from Oslo to Zürich crosses no seams.

Base scenery for nine countries

Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark, Germany, Switzerland, Austria, Italy and France at 30 m terrain resolution, with 3 700 airports: runways, taxiways and aprons from OpenStreetMap and OurAirports, PAPI and approach lights at night. 0.2–2 GB per country, installed from inside the app.

Photoreal add-ons (in rework)

Switzerland and Baden-Württemberg at 1 m: swissALTI3D terrain, SWISSIMAGE orthophotos, swissBUILDINGS3D with per-building meshes, swissTLM3D roads, rail and water, surveyed lake beds. The packs are being rebuilt for the new globe and are offline until then.

Six aircraft, one glider

Cessna 172, Pilatus PC-7, Fairchild A-10, the McDonnell Douglas MD-81 / MD-82 / MD-83 / MD-87 with 60 airline liveries, and the Schleicher ASK 21. The FlightGear ports fly with their own systems and clickable cockpits.

JSBSim flight dynamics

Full 6-DOF simulation for every airframe, with the ground model reading the scenery under each wheel: runway surfaces, slopes, plateaus.

Radio navigation

VOR, DME, NDB and ILS receivers with a worldwide navaid database, magnetic headings from the WMM2025 model, and the radios in the Cessna's panel.

Weather

Editable weather layers (wind, turbulence, visibility, QNH), live METAR weather from the nearest station, volumetric cloud layers, day/night sky with a time-of-day control.

VFR map frame

Heading-up MFD-style inset: hillshade terrain, range rings, airports, navaids and ILS feathers. Toggle with N, zoom 80–5 km.

Scenery editor & packs

Draw roads and railways on the terrain, place 3D objects with a Blender-style gizmo, bake it into your own scenery pack. A converter imports scenery packages from other simulators as object packs that sit on top of the base world.

Every control yours

SDL gamepad mapping, per-axis joystick calibration, and a full keyboard map you can rebind key by key in Settings → Keyboard.

Free & open source

Free software under the GNU GPL, version 2. Code on github.com/Q-coder/provpilot. Scenery under the respective free-geodata licenses (SRTM, OSM, OurAirports, swisstopo, LGL-BW); the ported aircraft under the GPL of FlightGear.

Installation

The app itself is a ~330 MB download with all aircraft on board. Scenery is installed from inside the app: the base packs are one small download per country (0.2–2 GB). The photoreal Swiss and Baden-Württemberg add-ons (~165 GB for full coverage) are being reworked and are not available at the moment.

macOS Apple Silicon

  1. Download the latest ProvPilot-x.y.z-arm64.dmg from the button at the top of this page.
  2. Open the DMG and drag ProvPilot.app into your Applications folder (or anywhere else).
  3. The build is signed and notarized, so first launch from Finder works without right-click → Open.
  4. On first launch, open Settings → Downloads and install the base pack of the countries you fly in — Switzerland is 0.2 GB, Germany 1.3 GB. Each one is flyable on its own.
  5. Pick an airport in the welcome screen and click Fly!

Requirements: macOS 12+, Apple Silicon (M1 or newer). Disk: a few GB for the base packs.

Windows x86_64

  1. Download the latest ProvPilot-Windows-x.y.z.zip from the button at the top of this page.
  2. Right-click the ZIP → Properties → tick Unblock → OK. Then extract it somewhere you can write to (e.g. C:\Users\<you>\ProvPilot\ — not Program Files).
  3. The build is not signed yet. Windows SmartScreen may show "Windows protected your PC" the first time. Click More info → Run anyway.
  4. Launch ProvPilot.exe.
  5. Open Settings → Downloads and install the base pack of the countries you fly in. Same two-tier model as on macOS.
  6. Pick an airport and click Fly!

Requirements: Windows 10/11 x64, dedicated GPU with Vulkan support recommended. Disk: a few GB for the base packs.

Trouble? Logs are written to %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\ProVPilot\logs\godot.log — send the file with any bug report.

Cockpit interaction

In the cockpit camera the Cessna's and PC-7's levers (mixture, throttle, flaps, magnetos) follow a mouse drag, and the FlightGear-ported A-10, MD-80 family and ASK 21 have fully clickable panels: switches, knobs, guards and levers. One rule everywhere — left button or wheel down turns a knob down or counter-clockwise, right button or wheel up turns it up or clockwise; Shift makes a knob coarse. Ctrl+Shift+H outlines every clickable area.

Half-axis joysticks (e.g. Logitech Dual Action) work too — rebind the offending axis in Settings → Joystick or fall back to the keyboard.

Scenery coverage

Two tiers. The base scenery is one pack per country: 30 m terrain, every airport with runways, taxiways, aprons and night lighting, and the Swiss road and rail network where it has been baked. It is small and flyable on its own. The photoreal add-ons for Switzerland and Baden-Württemberg (1 m terrain, orthophotos, buildings and trees) sit on top of it in the Scenery Library; they are being rebuilt for the new globe and are offline for now.

Use the in-app downloader: Settings → Downloads. Packs are sha256-verified and extracted to a folder of your choice.

Release notes

v1.1.0 — The sea, forests and city lights, MD-80 autoflight, open source on GitHub

November 2026

  • The sea — the OpenStreetMap coastline is cut into the base terrain of the seven coastal countries and one water surface reaches the horizon: a soft shore, sky reflection, sun glint and ripples that drift with the wind. Islands the height model lacks are completed from the coastline. Aircraft can ditch; a ditched aircraft sinks.
  • Forests and land cover — forests from OpenStreetMap in all nine countries, drawn as trees of seven species near the aircraft (conifers take over with altitude) and painted into the ground to the horizon together with farmland, towns, industry, rock, glaciers and sand. Roads and railways stay clear of trees.
  • Night — towns, industry and their streets are lit after dusk out to 45 km; haze and clouds darken with twilight; PAPI lights follow each runway's real glide path.
  • MD-80 autoflight — the DFGS flies: heading select and hold, VOR / LOC, ILS and autoland, altitude hold and capture, vertical speed, IAS / Mach, autothrottle with EPR limits and TOGA, the flight mode annunciators with their power-up test. The MCDU works as far as the FlightGear package goes, reverse thrust is on R, the landing and position lights sit where the real ones do.
  • ILS and navaids — the ILS catalog is corrected against current AIPs and OpenStreetMap (51 frequency and ident changes), parallel runways resolve to the right localizer, NAV 2 has DME.
  • Crash detection, pause and logbook — hard touchdowns, overstress, overspeed, water and overturned aircraft end the flight with a Restart / Resume dialog (Settings → Realism); P pauses; every flight is recorded in Settings → Logbook with totals and an altitude / speed profile.
  • VFR map — pan with the mouse, north-up or heading-up, a movable and resizable frame, the open sea in blue, and wide views that fill in a fraction of a second from prebuilt overview cells.
  • Aircraft — every aircraft rotates about its wheels and compresses its struts with the flight model; heat shimmer behind jet nozzles and contrails in cold air; clear canopy glass on the PC-7 and ASK 21; clouds with a new shape and lighting model.
  • Controls — every gamepad axis and button is bound in Settings → Joystick and can be left unassigned; Alt+Enter toggles fullscreen.
  • Base scenery packs v2 — all nine countries are republished with the coastline, forests, land cover and map overview data; Settings → Downloads offers them as updates.
  • Open source — ProVPilot is now released under the GNU GPL, version 2, with the complete source at github.com/Q-coder/provpilot. Every aircraft, model and data source is credited in the About dialog; the car is a new public-domain model.

v1.0.20 — A European globe, the MD-80 family, radio navigation

November 2026

  • The world is a globe — the runtime is now WGS84 ECEF end to end: no flat frame, no seams, the horizon curves correctly from the ground to orbit, and the intro zoom from space doubles as the loading view. Startup no longer parses hundreds of megabytes of terrain manifests.
  • Base scenery for nine countries — Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark, Germany, Switzerland, Austria, Italy and France at 30 m (SRTM), one small pack each (0.2–2 GB) in Settings → Downloads. 3 700 airports get runways, taxiways, aprons and guidance lines from OpenStreetMap and OurAirports, flattened plateaus in the terrain, and night lighting with PAPI, approach, edge and threshold lights (117 000 fixtures). The Swiss and Baden-Württemberg 1 m packs become add-ons on top. Extraction of the packs went from hours to seconds.
  • McDonnell Douglas MD-81 / MD-82 / MD-83 / MD-87 — a port of the FlightGear MD-80 (Josh Davidson) flying in JSBSim with its own electrical, fuel, hydraulic, pneumatic and flight-control systems, the engine variants of each model, the short-fuselage MD-87, a fully clickable cockpit, the original sound set and 60 airline liveries selectable in Settings → Aircraft.
  • Schleicher ASK 21 — a two-seat glider with a JSBSim glider model; an airborne start (height, speed, heading) in Settings → Aircraft until winch and tow exist. The throttle inputs work its airbrakes.
  • Pilatus PC-7 and Fairchild A-10 cockpits — the PC-7 gets a built instrument panel, the A-10 arrives as a full FlightGear port at real size with instruments, a clickable cockpit, electrical system, gear animation and lights. All aircraft are now drawn at their real size (the Cessna mesh was 14 % oversize).
  • Radio navigation — VOR, DME, NDB and ILS receivers with a worldwide navaid database (OurAirports, nav.dat ILS plus synthetic localizers), magnetic headings from the WMM2025 model, the KX165 NAV/COM, KR87 ADF, VOR and ADF heads in the Cessna's panel, the tuned station in the HUD, navaids and ILS feathers on the VFR map.
  • Weather — a Weather tab with editable layers (wind, turbulence, visibility, QNH) that drive JSBSim and the fog, live METAR weather from the nearest station (refetched as you travel), volumetric cloud layers, loadable weather profiles, a day/night terminator seen from space.
  • Scenery editor and scenery packs — F8 opens an editor on the terrain: draw roads and railways with curves and roundabouts, flatten plateaus, place and move 3D objects with a Blender-style gizmo, bake it all into your own scenery pack. Scenery packs stack in the Scenery Library by priority, with exclusion zones that hide the base world's buildings and trees under a bespoke airfield. A converter turns scenery packages from other simulators into such object packs.
  • Base roads and railways — a road and rail network baked into the base terrain from swissTLM3D (Switzerland) and OpenStreetMap: corridors cut into the terrain, embankments, bridges and tunnels, motorway junctions with lane markings, roundabouts, and a road surface the ground model reads under the wheels.
  • Vehicles — a car and an Re 4/4 locomotive in the welcome screen's Vehicles frame, driven on the baked roads and rails.
  • Controls — every key is listed and rebindable in Settings → Keyboard; joystick flight axes (elevator, aileron, rudder, throttle, brakes) are polled straight into the flight model; Shift+C cycles the cameras backwards; the right mouse button held pans every view; Ctrl+arrows move the eye point; Space + mouse looks around the cockpit.
  • Settings — a tabbed settings screen rebuilt in C++ with Aircraft (fuel, start conditions, liveries, reference speeds, live centre of gravity), Scenery, Downloads, Graphics, World, Weather, Map, Sound, Realism, Joystick and Keyboard tabs.
  • Under the hood — the game logic moved from GDScript into the C++ extension; Windows and macOS share one export configuration, so the FlightGear ports, their sounds and liveries ship identically on both.

v1.0.10 — VFR map frame

September 2026

  • Heading-up VFR map inset — a new MFD-style map frame in the HUD, rendered entirely in C++: cartographic hillshade terrain with hypsometric tint, range rings and a km scale bar, airport symbols with ICAO labels, and canton borders — all rotating with your heading. Toggle it with N and zoom from 80 km down to 5 km view width with the mouse wheel or −/+. The map keeps tracking the aircraft while you slew, too.
  • VFR Map settings — a new Settings → VFR Map section shows/hides the frame and picks its size (Small / Medium / Large). Your zoom level is remembered between sessions.
  • Map data ships in the base scenery packs — the republished base packs (v2) now carry ~150 MB of pre-rendered hillshade tiles per region, so the in-app VFR map works out of the box for downloader installs — including Baden-Württemberg, where cells straddling the CH/BW border are rendered from both regions' elevation data.
  • Final A-10 model — existing installs automatically re-extract the updated Fairchild A-10A Thunderbolt II on first launch (aircraft bundle 1.0.11).
  • macOS release build hardened — the macOS app now ships the release-mode world3d engine and building faces data, with every GDExtension verified at build time.

v1.0.9 — A-10 Thunderbolt II, day/night sky, lake bathymetry

September 2026

  • Fairchild A-10A Thunderbolt II — a third aircraft: the twin-engine close-air-support jet with its own JSBSim flight model (2× GE TF34 turbofans) and animated retractable gear. Engine commands now drive all engines together, so the twin runs symmetrically.
  • Day/night sun & atmospheric sky — a single-scattering atmosphere shader keeps the horizon matched to the globe's curvature at every altitude (no more flat sky above ~90 km), with a curved limb from orbit. The sun swings through the day, moving the scene light and shadows with it; Settings → World adds a time-of-day control (system clock or manual HH:MM) and the HUD shows the local time.
  • Lakes have real depth — swisstopo's surveyed lake beds (swissBATHY3D) are now carved into the terrain as sparse overlays: the whole international Bodensee, 523 km² of bed down to 251 m. The pipeline supports all 22 lakes swisstopo publishes.
  • Water bake repair — large open-water tiles had been baked at nonsense heights (the middle of the Bodensee was a single quad with corners at 171–330 m instead of the true water level). Repaired per connected water body: corrupt-triangle tiles dropped from 395 to 303.
  • Daytime horizon fixed — the horizon rendered sunset-yellow at every hour because atmospheric attenuation charged each sample its own full optical depth. Noon is now a neutral pale haze, evenings stay orange, and the sun disk reappears when you climb.
  • Swiss military airports — the six Swiss Air Force airfields (Dübenendorf LSMD, Emmen LSME, Alpnach LSMA, Mollis LSMF, Meiringen LSMM, Payerne LSMP) join the airport catalog with per-runway-end takeoff positions.
  • Scenery packs v2 — the Switzerland base pack now bundles the lake-bed overlay and the water rebake; the Baden-Württemberg base carries updated terrain/orthophoto and its detail quadrants include a building refresh. The in-app downloader picks up new pack versions automatically on Refresh.

v1.0.8 — Scenery precision pass: draping, alignment, distant terrain

June 2026

  • Rivers & lakes no longer drape or float — Swiss water bodies that used to hover above (or cut through) the ground now sit flush on the terrain. The runtime drape heuristic was mis-flagging legitimately-sloped Alpine rivers as bake errors; it now inspects each triangle individually, so genuine sloped water stays put while the rare corrupt tile is still corrected.
  • Roads & railways hug the ground — baked roads were rendered a fixed step above the bare-earth terrain, so at taxi height the aircraft appeared to drive underneath them. Roads are now dropped to sit flush, while bridges and embankments keep their real height.
  • Aircraft sits correctly on the terrain — the near-field terrain mesh now renders at full resolution and block-averages coarser tiles, so the visible ground matches the surface the flight model rides on. No more sinking into, or hovering above, the ground on steep terrain.
  • Distant scenery is back — the low-resolution mountain panorama (e.g. the Alps seen from the northern plateau) is rendered again. A streaming bug was silently discarding every far-terrain chunk before it could load; the horizon now fills in properly.
  • Terrain Distance setting now works — the Settings → Terrain Distance slider was previously saved but never applied. It now controls how far the full-detail terrain ring extends.
  • Orthophoto tile seams removed — photoreal tiles are now clamped at their edges, eliminating the thin seams that appeared between adjacent tiles at distance.
  • macOS: buildings, roads and rivers render again — the 1.0.7 macOS build shipped an out-of-date scenery engine, so the release app showed only terrain and orthophotos. The build pipeline now always rebuilds the scenery engine from source, so 3D buildings, roads/rail and rivers & lakes appear for both Switzerland and Baden-Württemberg.

v1.0.6 — Pilatus PC-7, retractable gear animation, rolling wheels

May 2026

  • Pilatus PC-7 Turbo Trainer — a second aircraft joins the Cessna 172. Swiss-built tandem trainer with its own JSBSim flight model, cockpit and exterior, selectable from the welcome screen.
  • Animated retractable landing gear — the PC-7 gear now plays a proper Blender retract/extend animation (legs fold, then the nose-wheel bay doors close). Toggle with G; the cycle holds at the end and only reverses on the next toggle.
  • Rolling wheels — the PC-7 main and nose wheels now spin up with ground speed (driven by JSBSim) and stop once airborne, with correct per-wheel rotation direction. Nose-wheel steering tracks the rudder on the ground.
  • Scenery trees — low-poly vegetation is now part of the distribution.
  • Windows scenery now matches macOS — the Windows portable build previously started over empty terrain and was missing buildings, roads, railways and water. The streaming tile manager now reads the current 2 m/px terrain tiles correctly, and the building, road and water managers are always created so the runtime scenery library populates them on Windows. Terrain, photoreal orthophotos, 3D buildings, roads/rail and rivers & lakes all load for both Switzerland and Baden-Württemberg.

v1.0.4 — Scenery distribution, in-app downloader, Windows portable build

May 2026

  • In-app scenery downloader — Settings → Download Scenery fetches base + detail quadrants from md80.ch, verifies sha256 and extracts into a folder you pick. No more manual ZIP wrangling. Direct URLs are also listed below the coverage map for offline / mirror use.
  • Portable install — shared provpilot.cfg next to the executable, on-disk aircraft tree, and a runtime GLB load-from-PCK fallback. Drop the folder anywhere (or onto a USB stick) and it just runs; settings travel with it.
  • Windows portable build — the Windows ZIP is now a self-contained portable install with the same layout as macOS. Extract anywhere writable, double-click ProvPilot.exe.
  • Memory-leak fix in the streaming tile manager — rapid airport teleports no longer leak chunk-zone tiles. Previously tiles.size() would climb from ~10k to >30k after a handful of jumps, dragging FPS into single digits and inflating elev_db, render objects and GPU memory unboundedly. The chunk-zone unload path now also drops tiles whose covering chunk is no longer tracked.
  • Bodensee water flattened — baked water tiles whose vertex Y-span exceeds 30 m (a known bake bug in the Bodensee interior quads, where a single 4-vertex polygon shipped with corners from +5 m to +269 m) are now re-draped from the elevation database at runtime. The dark wing over Altenrhein is gone.
  • 2 GiB download fix — the downloader now uses the on-disk file size for progress, working around an HTTPRequest int32 overflow that broke progress display past 2 GiB.
  • Continue Flight — the welcome screen now offers a green Continue button to jump back into your last session after pressing Esc.
  • About dialog with full credits and third-party licenses (Godot 4.6, JSBSim, Scenery3D, swisstopo, LGL-BW, OpenStreetMap, Cessna 172 model).
  • Scenery split into per-region base packs and four detail quadrants — install only the corners you fly in.
  • Welcome and settings screens now anchor and scale to the full viewport, so they work on 4K and ultrawide monitors.
  • HUD labels reorganised into a clean bottom row; cleared hard-coded dev-machine scenery paths from node_3d.tscn.
  • Per-second diagnostic snapshot in the log (jump count, tile / elev_db / chunk counts, GPU memory) for fast triage of regressions.
  • Project bumped to 1.0.4, version label now read from project.godot at runtime.
